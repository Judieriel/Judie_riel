Case Study — Retail Analytics

Nova Gear Store: Revenue, Markets & Loss Analysis

A 5-year retail transaction analysis across 8 countries — examining revenue trends, market behavior, and where the business is losing money.

ToolMicrosoft Excel
Records9,850 orders (cleaned)
Markets8 countries · 5 years
Introduction

This capstone project analyzes a Nova Gear retail dataset containing transaction records across 8 countries over a 5-year period. The goal was to understand revenue performance, customer behavior across markets, and identify where the business was losing money.

Objectives
  1. Revenue trend by market — examine how revenue changed across the 8 markets over 5 years and identify which markets are growing or declining.
  2. Market behavior — determine whether customers across the 8 markets behave as one unified market or as 8 distinct markets.
  3. Revenue loss — identify where the business is losing money, quantify the losses, and pinpoint which markets and products are most responsible.

Key KPIs
Total Revenue
Sum of revenue from completed orders only — refunds and cancellations excluded.
Revenue by Country
Total revenue generated per market, tracked across the 5-year period.
Order Completion Rate
Share of orders successfully completed vs. cancelled or refunded.
Revenue Loss
Total value lost to cancellations and refunds, broken down by country and product.
Order Volume by Country
Number of orders placed per market — a measure of market activity.
Loss by Product
Combined refund + cancellation value per product — flags the most problematic SKUs.
Findings & Insights
1. What changed? Revenue by country and over time
Total revenue grew from $689,219 (2021) to $1,497,777 (2025) — more than double
The USA remained the largest and fastest-growing market by absolute value, rising from $209,193 to $432,099. But the more striking story is Nigeria, which grew nearly 6x (from $34,742 to $218,384), and Kenya, which followed a similar trajectory (from $31,811 to $176,473) — making them the fastest-growing emerging markets. Germany was the only market in decline, dropping 45% from $136,268 to $75,080, with the decline beginning in 2023. China grew overall but dipped in 2024 before recovering, suggesting some instability worth monitoring. 2023 stands out as a turning point — the year Nigeria and Kenya accelerated while Germany began its consistent decline.
2. Do the 8 markets behave like one market, or eight?
8 distinct markets — confirmed by differing order volume, spend, and return behavior
The USA dominates order volume with 2,567 orders — the most active market by transaction count. Overall, 91.98% of orders were completed successfully, with 8.02% cancelled or refunded. Notably, the USA also recorded the highest rate of refunds and cancellations of any market across all 5 years — meaning the market driving the most volume is also carrying the highest revenue-loss risk. These differences in spending behavior, order volume, and return rates confirm the 8 markets do not behave uniformly and should be treated as 8 distinct markets, not one.
3. Where is the business losing money?
$372,264 lost total — Cancellations ($215,799) outweigh Refunds ($156,465)
Cancellations being the larger share suggests checkout abandonment is a bigger problem than product dissatisfaction after purchase. At the country level, the USA recorded the highest loss at $112,848 — 30% of all losses across the 8 markets — despite (or because of) being the top revenue generator. At the product level, the Dell XPS 13 had the highest combined loss at $51,063, making it the most problematic product in the dataset. The MacBook Air M2 showed a different pattern: low refunds ($3,567) but very high cancellations ($27,855) — suggesting customers want the product but abandon it at checkout, likely due to its premium price point.
Recommendations
  • Shift focus and budget away from Germany toward Nigeria and Kenya, which show the strongest growth potential.
  • Treat each of the 8 markets as distinct — develop separate marketing and pricing strategies rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.
  • Investigate USA checkout abandonment specifically, since it drives the most revenue but also the most loss.
  • Reduce Dell XPS 13 supply by at least 50% to limit exposure from its high combined loss.
  • Introduce flexible payment options for premium products like the MacBook Air M2 to reduce cart/checkout abandonment.
Limitations
No cost column was available in the dataset, so gross profit could not be fully calculated — this analysis is based on revenue and loss figures only.
Conclusion

Revenue across the 8 markets more than doubled over 5 years, from $689,219 to $1,497,777, with Nigeria and Kenya emerging as the fastest-growing markets. However, the business lost $372,264 to cancellations and refunds, with the USA — its top market — accounting for 30% of all losses. Addressing checkout abandonment, protecting high-growth markets, and managing problematic products like the Dell XPS 13 will be key to sustaining growth going forward.
