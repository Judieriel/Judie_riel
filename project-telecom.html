Case Study — Telecom Analytics

Customer Churn & Revenue Analysis

A full KPI-driven analysis of a Nigerian telecom customer dataset — cleaning, metric design, and business insight, built entirely in Excel.

ToolMicrosoft Excel
Customers520
FocusChurn & Retention
Objectives
  1. Revenue by plan — determine which subscription plan generates the highest revenue, to identify the most profitable product offering.
  2. Churn by segment — identify the customer segment(s) with the highest churn rate, to pinpoint groups most at risk of attrition.
  3. Churn drivers — examine the factors associated with customer churn, to inform targeted retention strategies.
Dashboard
Key KPIs
Total Customers
Baseline size of the customer base — denominator for every rate-based KPI.
Total Monthly Revenue
Sum of monthly spend across all customers — core financial performance metric.
Average Tenure
Average months a customer stays — a proxy for loyalty and relationship length.
Revenue Lost to Churn
Monthly revenue tied to churned customers — the direct financial cost of attrition.
Retention Rate
Share of customers still active — the inverse of churn rate.
Churn Rate by Segment
Churn broken down by plan, age group, NPS status, and complaint category.
Findings & Insights
Which plan generates the most revenue?
Postpaid — ₦4.0M, ahead of Data Only (₦3.8M) and Prepaid (₦3.3M)
Postpaid is the most valuable segment by total contribution, with Data Only close behind — signaling strong demand for data-centric plans worth investing in further.
Which segment churns the most?
Mature Adults (45–54) — highest churn rate of any age group
Older, typically loyal customers churning most is unusual — pointing to a pricing, service, or plan-fit issue rather than general market volatility. This group carries higher spend, so losing them likely costs more.
What factors are associated with churn?
Poor coverage (top complaint) · Passive NPS customers (28.7% churn) · Long-tenure customers (26.8 months avg.)
Churn isn't driven by new or vocal customers — it's driven by established, seemingly satisfied customers who disengage quietly. Coverage is the leading complaint tied to churn, and Passives churn more than Detractors because they never raise a complaint before leaving.
Recommendations
  • Protect the Postpaid segment and grow Data Only offerings, while reviewing Prepaid's pricing and bundle value.
  • Build loyalty touchpoints for customers approaching the 24-month mark, before value fatigue sets in.
  • Prioritize Mature Adults for personalized retention — plan reviews, loyalty discounts, service check-ins.
  • Invest in network coverage in affected regions — the leading churn-linked complaint is a product issue, not a service one.
  • Proactively engage Passive NPS customers through check-ins and small loyalty nudges, since they give no warning before leaving.
Conclusion

Churn in this dataset isn't mainly caused by new or unhappy customers who complain — it's driven by long-term customers who quietly leave without warning. This shifts the recommended strategy from reacting to complaints toward proactively monitoring tenure, NPS trends, and engagement. Improving network coverage and rewarding long-term customers are the two highest-impact actions to reduce churn and protect revenue.
